## The Notes Between Us

*The Notes Between Us* is my current novel project set in the MAGIS universe, a world where music is more than sound — it’s power, identity, and connection. This page will grow as the book does, collecting character notes, worldbuilding details, and excerpts.

### About the Story

*The Notes Between Us* follows Basil O’Brian, a music‑obsessed, eyeliner‑smudged, My‑Chemical‑Romance‑devoted teenager who’s convinced the world doesn’t really “get” him — until a mysterious envelope arrives in the mail. Inside is a song that shouldn’t exist and an invitation to MAGIS: Misfits Achieving Greatness in Song, a school where musical talent isn’t just encouraged, it’s amplified into something strange, powerful, and a little bit magical.

Basil’s mom is thrilled. His dad is skeptical. But the letter is real, the opportunity is huge, and the brand‑new guitar his dad buys him feels like a quiet vote of confidence he didn’t expect.

At MAGIS, Basil meets the bandmates who will become his closest friends — a group of weird, brilliant, chaotic musicians who push him creatively, challenge him emotionally, and force him to figure out who he is when he’s not hiding behind sarcasm and a band tee. As Basil learns how MAGIS works, he discovers that music here isn’t just performance; it’s identity, connection, and a force that can shape the world around him.

The story blends humor, heart, and musical magic — exploring creativity, belonging, and what happens when a kid who’s always felt like an outsider finally finds the place where he fits.

This section introduces the main cast of *The Notes Between Us* — Basil, his family, his bandmates, and the faculty who shape his journey at MAGIS.

### Basil O’Brian
Basil is a music‑obsessed, eyeliner‑smudged teenager who lives in a world scored by My Chemical Romance and late‑night guitar riffs. He’s dramatic in the way only a sixteen‑year‑old can be — convinced he’s misunderstood, convinced no one “gets” him, convinced his emotions are louder and heavier than everyone else’s. He hides behind sarcasm, band tees, and the comfort of knowing exactly which MCR track fits his mood at any given moment.

He’s talented, but he doesn’t think of himself that way. He plays because he *has* to — because music is the only place where his thoughts stop spiraling and his feelings make sense. He’s the kind of kid who will spend an hour perfecting a chord progression and then shrug like it was nothing. Creativity is instinct for him, not performance.

At home, he’s surrounded by a family that loves him in different ways:
- a mom who supports him unconditionally,
- a dad who wants him to follow a more “practical” path,
- and a little sister climbing the kitchen counters while he tries to eat breakfast.

He feels all of it — the support, the pressure, the chaos — even when he pretends he doesn’t.

The MAGIS letter hits him like a lightning strike. It’s validation he didn’t know he needed, proof that his weirdness, his intensity, his musical obsession might actually *mean* something. He’s skeptical, excited, terrified, and hopeful all at once. And when his dad buys him that guitar — that moment becomes a quiet emotional pivot he’ll never admit out loud.

At MAGIS, Basil is still Basil: awkward, sarcastic, self‑doubting, and trying way too hard not to try too hard. But he finds people who match his rhythm — Colton, Tyrone, Sam — and suddenly he’s not the lone emo kid in the corner anymore. He’s part of something. A band. A group. A world where music isn’t just expression; it’s identity, connection, and power.

Basil’s arc isn’t about saving the world. It’s about learning how to belong in it.
Learning how to trust people.
Learning how to trust himself.
Learning how to stop hiding behind noise and start making something real.

He’s messy, emotional, funny, insecure, talented, loyal, and deeply human — the kind of protagonist readers root for because they recognize pieces of themselves in him.

### Family

#### Mom
Basil’s endlessly supportive mother — the kind of parent who listens fully, encourages gently, and believes in her son with a sincerity that never feels forced. She’s always willing to hear Basil out, always ready to tell him things will be alright, and always able to soften the edges of a rough day. She’s not naive; she knows when a song is off‑key or when Basil’s emotions are running high. But she’s the type who would still smile at a terrible performance and say, oh my honey, that was just lovely, because she cares more about his courage than his perfection. Her presence brings warmth, steadiness, and a quiet confidence to Basil’s world — the emotional safety net he doesn’t always realize he needs.

#### Dad
Basil’s father — a finance‑industry lifer who starts his mornings with the Wall Street Journal and ends them tracking futures, market volatility, and whatever the S&P 500 decided to do that day. He speaks in stock terms Basil doesn’t understand, casually referencing portfolios, capital gains, and long‑term strategy as if everyone thinks that way. He’s dead‑set on Basil following in his footsteps someday, maybe even running a firm together, and he carries that hope quietly but firmly.

He’s slower to please, more reserved with praise, and not always sure how to connect with Basil’s emo‑kid, guitar‑centric world. But once he realizes music isn’t a phase — it’s Basil’s passion — he reacts fast and decisively. His gift of the guitar becomes a turning point, a rare moment of vulnerability and belief. And beneath the finance‑guy exterior, he’s a secret Creedence Clearwater Revival fan, humming along to “Have You Ever Seen the Rain” when he thinks no one’s listening.

#### Sis
Basil’s three‑to‑four‑year‑old sister — energetic, curious, and constantly in motion. She doesn’t have speaking lines in the story, but she’s a vivid presence in the O’Brian household. When Basil comes into the kitchen, she’s the one Mom gently wrangles with a promise of pancakes, keeping her from climbing across counters and cabinets. Her small, chaotic energy adds warmth and realism to Basil’s home life, a reminder that even in a house full of music and tension, there’s still a little kid trying to scale the kitchen like it’s a jungle gym.

### Bandmates

#### Colton
Basil’s upright‑bass‑playing friend from back home. Steady, rhythmic, and quietly brilliant, Colton brings a grounded musical presence to the band. He’s the kind of guy who taps out patterns on the table without realizing it, and the kind of friend who shows up even when he doesn’t know what to say.

#### Tyrone
Sharp‑eyed, sharp‑minded, and always keeping time with something only he can hear. Tyrone’s internal rhythm never shuts off. He’s confident without being cocky, intense without being intimidating, and becomes one of Basil’s closest friends almost by accident.

#### Sam
Warm, bright, and effortlessly musical. Sam hums melodies under her breath that sound like chart‑toppers, even when she’s not trying. She’s supportive without being soft, honest without being harsh, and becomes a key emotional anchor in Basil’s MAGIS experience.

### MAGIS Faculty

#### Ms. Bass
Precise, disciplined, and sharper than the edge of a metronome. Ms. Bass teaches theory and foundational technique, and she expects excellence from every student who walks into her classroom. Her approval is something you earn, not something you’re given.

#### Mr. Forte
Loud, energetic, and impossible to ignore. Mr. Forte treats every lesson like a performance, booming enthusiasm across the room whether students are ready for it or not. He pushes Basil and his classmates to play bigger, think bigger, and take risks they wouldn’t take on their own.

#### Fogerty
The headmaster of MAGIS — mythic, grounded, and carrying the kind of presence that makes a room go quiet without him asking. Fogerty’s intro speech sets the tone for the entire school: you’re here for a reason, anyone can make noise, but the chosen few make magic.

#### Buckethead
The enigmatic guitar instructor whose teaching style is equal parts brilliance and chaos. Buckethead communicates more through riffs than words, and his lessons often feel like puzzles Basil has to solve. He’s unpredictable, intimidating, and occasionally hilarious — the kind of mentor who forces students to discover things they didn’t know they were capable of.
